<blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">Just imagine how sales will increase when our customers can finally confirm their orders with <br>just one click!</blockquote><p>I picture Jeff Bezos giving this speech to his army of tech geeks to motivate them to produce <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-Click">Amazon's 1-Click patent</a>.</p><p>There is immense power in formulating requirements in the form of classic storytelling: "<em>Those who tell stories rule society,</em>" said Plato. </p><p>That is also true for <em>Product Managers</em> and <em>Product Owners</em> that want to rule their backlogs. Let us move past the "<em>There should be a button that when clicked...</em>" requirement style. That's just old and very IBM-style.</p><p>Shall we try a new approach? </p><p>One that originates at the very dawn of history when our fellow cavemen spent their nights painting their hunting stories on the walls? In our dreams, we can create images, sounds, and sensations that go far beyond the capability of any entertainment industry. Release your brakes, and your brain will take you on a joy ride.</p><p>Storytelling, specifically the "<em>Just imagine</em>" opening, triggers our brain into a relaxed and creative mode. In his book "<a href="https://www.amazon.com/Exactly-What-Say-Influence-Impact-ebook/dp/B073SF65ZZ"><em>Exactly What to Say,</em></a>" Phil M. Jones dedicates an entire chapter to these two words, explaining how stories are the building blocks of getting to know the world around us.</p><p>Stories beat facts because they convey feelings and emotions. And we often make decisions based on how we feel; with few exceptions, logic always comes in second place.</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt"><em>Those who tell stories rule society</em><br>– Plato </blockquote><p><em>Just imagine</em> a backlog that compels your team to unravel a possible future that brings peace and stability to the Galaxy! Of course, you can not start every user story with the acclaimed "<em>Long time ago, in a Galaxy far away...</em>" – Lucas' wouldn't be happy about it.</p><h2 id="back-to-the-future">Back to The Future</h2><p>A good story is like a time machine. But while many childhood tales bring us back to a "<em>Long time ago, far far away,</em>" professional storytelling can travel us into the near future. </p><p>A future in which we have already conquered our goals and benefit from the expected results. A good story can make us experience today the winnings of tomorrow.</p><p>And by winnings, I mean gaining something new as well as protecting an existing value: "<em>Just imagine our competitor implementing FeatureX before us, taking customers away from our App.</em>"</p><p>Experience the future firsthand is a powerful motivator. People get to action to preserve what they have, so when you give them a direct experience of the future, they don't want to lose it. It's already theirs, and they will work their asses off to preserve such an experience.</p><p>People work hard to build the Future they experience through storytelling as it was a self-fulfilling prophecy from an ancient Shaolin Monk legend.</p><h2 id="freedom-of-choice">Freedom of Choice</h2><p>Writing a compelling story is all about generating a good contrast between the initial situation and the final expected result.</p><p>Bruce Willis always starts as a poor bastard with a miserable life. But in the end, he is back with his wife in Die Hard, gets to date the living Fifth Element, and he even gets to kiss Jessica Alba in Sincity!</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">A good story is all about generating contrast.</blockquote><p>When you adopt storytelling in your backlog and focus on selling a final situation so much better than the starting point, you naturally fly over implementation details. The details are boring. We want the action, not the details!</p><p>When your team embarks on the journey that bridges the present to the future that you sold them, it's their job to figure out the details. <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKorP55Aqvg">They are the experts</a>.</p><p>We talk so much about giving freedom to the teams. </p><p>Storytelling implements this freedom by asking you, the PO, to focus on the result, not the journey.</p><h2 id="write-once-sell-many-times">Write once, Sell many times.</h2><p>Because you happily fly over any detail, your story will be generic enough to be served to different groups of people.</p><p>Take this story:</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">Just imagine how sales will increase when our customers can finally confirm their orders with <br>just one click!</blockquote><p>You can serve this to your design, frontend, backend, and data-science team without changing anything. The story sells a possible increase in sales and binds that to a better user experience. </p><p>How to reach that result?</p><p>Ask your teams.</p><p>Plural.</p><p>Give the same story to different teams and let them figure out how to get there. Help them coordinate their efforts, then get out of the way.</p><p>Start working you your next good story!</p>
