<p>In a <a href="__GHOST_URL__/microdomains">Micro-Domains Architecture</a>, you are going to deal with multiple services, each one exposed with its own <em>URL</em>. Actually, you don't even get to know that. It's likely just local ports in <em>local development</em> and URLs in <em>staging</em> and <em>production</em>. </p><p>Anyhow, not only do you have multiple target URLs, but those may vary in each environment. And in time.</p><div class="kg-card kg-callout-card kg-callout-card-blue"><div class="kg-callout-emoji">📌</div><div class="kg-callout-text">And don't forget that each service <strong>must be considered entirely isolated</strong> from the rest of the App. Its team may decide to move it to a different URL entirely, at any time, for any reason.</div></div><p>Shortly, we can't use absolute URLs or environment-specific configuration files. It just won't fly. </p><p>So, who are you gonna call?</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1644242833303-5128457d5d47?crop=entropy&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;fit=max&amp;fm=jpg&amp;ixid=MnwxMTc3M3wwfDF8c2VhcmNofDF8fGdob3N0YnVzdGVyfGVufDB8fHx8MTY1MzYzMDM2OA&amp;ixlib=rb-1.2.1&amp;q=80&amp;w=2000" class="kg-image" alt loading="lazy" width="5472" height="3648" srcset="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1644242833303-5128457d5d47?crop=entropy&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;fit=max&amp;fm=jpg&amp;ixid=MnwxMTc3M3wwfDF8c2VhcmNofDF8fGdob3N0YnVzdGVyfGVufDB8fHx8MTY1MzYzMDM2OA&amp;ixlib=rb-1.2.1&amp;q=80&amp;w=600 600w, https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1644242833303-5128457d5d47?crop=entropy&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;fit=max&amp;fm=jpg&amp;ixid=MnwxMTc3M3wwfDF8c2VhcmNofDF8fGdob3N0YnVzdGVyfGVufDB8fHx8MTY1MzYzMDM2OA&amp;ixlib=rb-1.2.1&amp;q=80&amp;w=1000 1000w, https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1644242833303-5128457d5d47?crop=entropy&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;fit=max&amp;fm=jpg&amp;ixid=MnwxMTc3M3wwfDF8c2VhcmNofDF8fGdob3N0YnVzdGVyfGVufDB8fHx8MTY1MzYzMDM2OA&amp;ixlib=rb-1.2.1&amp;q=80&amp;w=1600 1600w, https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1644242833303-5128457d5d47?crop=entropy&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;fit=max&amp;fm=jpg&amp;ixid=MnwxMTc3M3wwfDF8c2VhcmNofDF8fGdob3N0YnVzdGVyfGVufDB8fHx8MTY1MzYzMDM2OA&amp;ixlib=rb-1.2.1&amp;q=80&amp;w=2400 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption>Who are you gonna call? It is the only parameter that is actually given to any service in the system. </p><p>Services in the App will all look at the Service Registry for two reasons:</p><ol><li>They'll ask for the URL of any other service in the system</li><li>They'll promptly communicate changes about their own location</li></ol><p><strong>🧐 </strong><em>Is this much different from a Service Discovery in a Micro-Service architecture?</em></p><p>Not at all! It's practically the same goal, but at different abstraction levels. </p><p><strong>A <em>Service Discovery</em> keeps a list of running instances of a service</strong>. A load-balancer uses a Service Discovery to figure out where to send traffic. As containers come and go, or as the system scales up and down, the Service Discovery information promptly changes and must be pushed to whoever is interested.</p><p><strong>A <em>Service Registry's</em> targets point to a DNS</strong>. Behind that, there may be the need for a <em>Service Discovery</em> as well based on the target's internal architecture. The information stored in a Service Registry changes very seldom but must be propagated very swiftly, for there is no alternate destination where to send traffic to.</p><p>Also, publishing new <em>Service IDs + Target</em> must be a <strong>bottom-up activity in order to avoid governance bottlenecks</strong>, but the propagation of such information has to be top-down in order to guarantee local caching and better performances.</p><p>So a Service Registry must be a composable and distributed service.</p>
