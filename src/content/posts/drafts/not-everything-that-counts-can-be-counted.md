<p>One year and a half ago I landed my first management job. The fancy title was Chief Technology Officer. In reality, I've been working together with great people to build a team that simply wasn't there, and save a product that was doomed to crash and burn.</p><div class="kg-card kg-callout-card kg-callout-card-yellow"><div class="kg-callout-emoji">👉</div><div class="kg-callout-text">This is the story how I got into my first two hiring rounds, the mistakes I did, the lessons I've learned.</div></div><hr><h2 id="round-one">Round One</h2><p>Over the first few months, I had to run an insanely quick hiring spree, forced by the budget-related timeline imposed by upper management. </p><p>Out of the first 5 hirings I got:</p><ul><li>two great people</li><li>one a total disaster</li><li>the rest were so-so.</li></ul><p>We ran a new hiring round seven months later. We onboarded three Engineers and one Scrum Master. Four straight hits. Bullseye. A sheer success.</p><blockquote>What changed over those seven months, as so to boost our hiring success rate by 66%?</blockquote><p>I'm going to answer the question by sharing my experience. But the short version of it lies with <a href="https://quoteinvestigator.com/2010/05/26/everything-counts-einstein/">William Bruce Cameron's 1963 quote</a>:</p><blockquote><strong><strong>Not everything that can be counted counts</strong></strong><br><strong><strong>Not everything that counts can be counted</strong></strong> </blockquote><hr><h2 id="round-one-1">Round One</h2><p>During my first hiring round, I played by the rules. </p><p>I read through CVs <em>matching the candidate's keywords with our tech stack</em>, considered years of experience, and the good names of previous job experiences.</p><p>After screening the candidates with a coding test that made my colleagues happy, I ran the candidate through one or two live interviews that were revolving around <em>architectural questions and war games</em> concocted to evaluate the candidate's hard skills.</p><blockquote>I was simply trying to measure the candidate by objective and unbiased characteristics.</blockquote><p>The two guys that scored the best, revealed themselves for being the worst. </p>
