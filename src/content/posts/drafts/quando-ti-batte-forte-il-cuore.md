<p>Molte persone affette da fibrillazione atriale e non se ne accorgono. Io sono tra i fortunati che hanno avuto sintomi chiari e <strong>i sintomi sono suggerimenti importanti che portano a una diagnosi e a un piano di trattamento</strong> della malattia.</p><p>Vorrei iniziare questo articolo condividendo lo strumento che mi ha dato la prima indicazione concreta di Fibrillazione Atriale: <strong>l'Apple Watch</strong>.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/apple-watch-red.png" class="kg-image" alt loading="lazy" width="2000" height="732" srcset="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w600/2021/11/apple-watch-red.png 600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1000/2021/11/apple-watch-red.png 1000w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1600/2021/11/apple-watch-red.png 1600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/apple-watch-red.png 2180w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption>The Apple Watch - rosso é meglio 😎</figcaption></figure><p>L'ho già elogiato in altri articoli, e mi sento un po' così ad insistere, poiché mi potresti fraintendere e pensare che io sia un rappresentante della Apple che riceve una provvigione. Non è così. Non sono affiliato con Apple, sono solo un cliente soddisfatto.</p><p>L'Apple Watch, versione 5 e successive, può eseguire un ECG semplice ma accurato. Inoltre, monitora il battito cardiaco durante il sonno, registrando eventuali segni di Fibrillazione Atriale. Non solo, ci sono funzionalità più interessanti che ho iniziato ad amare anche se sono secondarie ai miei scopi relativi alla salute.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/sinus-rhythm-2.png" class="kg-image" alt loading="lazy" width="1026" height="227" srcset="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w600/2021/11/sinus-rhythm-2.png 600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1000/2021/11/sinus-rhythm-2.png 1000w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/sinus-rhythm-2.png 1026w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption>Apple Watch ECG</figcaption></figure><h2 id="come-ci-si-sente-con-la-fibrillazione-atriale">Come ci si sente con la Fibrillazione Atriale?</h2><p>Risponderò a questa domanda, ma ricorda che questa è solamente la mia esperienza personale. Non sono un medico e quello che scrivo non sono indicazioni esaustive di Fibrillazione Atriale.</p><p>Riporto due principali senzazioni:</p><ul><li>Un battito mancato</li><li>Crisi di palpitazione o battito molto accelerato</li></ul><h2 id="il-battico-mancato">Il Battico Mancato</h2><p>Questa sensazione si accumula intorno allo sterno e di solito dura a lungo. Anche ore consecutive. Sento un senso generale di pressione con occasionali depressioni al centro del petto.</p><p>Quando misuro il battito al polso o al collo, sento come se ci fosse una pausa nei battiti del mio cuore. Ma verificando con l'ECG di Apple Watch, vedo battiti cardiaci doppi o tripli, assieme a sequenze di battiti regolari.</p><p>Quando sono in questo stato, l'ECG spesso risulta inconcludente. Quando ciò avviene, probabilmente sto per subire una crisi di battito accelerato.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/missed-heartbeat-sensation.png" class="kg-image" alt loading="lazy" width="2000" height="529" srcset="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w600/2021/11/missed-heartbeat-sensation.png 600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1000/2021/11/missed-heartbeat-sensation.png 1000w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1600/2021/11/missed-heartbeat-sensation.png 1600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/missed-heartbeat-sensation.png 2080w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption>Apple Watch ECG when I feel a "missed heartbeat"</figcaption></figure><p>Il più delle volte, questa sensazione dura per ore senza che si sviluppi una crisi. Vado avanti con la mia vita regolarmente e faccio del mio meglio per non dare troppo peso a questo disagio.</p><p>Occasionalmente riesco a liberarmene eseguendo esercizi di respirazione mirati. Ma non sempre funziona. Anche lo sport aiuta, sono riuscito ad uscire da questo stato semplicemente eseguendo alcuni squat. Ma non ho ancora identificato una soluzione stabile al problema.</p><p>Initially, I believed it was stress or anxiety. People around me, especially my mom, insist that I am very stressed by my work. I don’t really feel any kind of stress-related triggers, but who knows? I’m working this angle with a therapist with the goal of assessing my stress level.</p><p>So far, according to my Danish cardiologist, stress should be ruled out as a trigger.</p><h2 id="racing-heart-crisis">Racing Heart Crisis</h2><p>This is the bad guy in town. And the symptom that can potentially deliver a stroke. I’m always a bit tense - truly scared - when this happens.</p><p>It always starts in the ears, I feel the heartbeat pressure in the ears. Sometimes, I also feel it on my fingertips. Only a few seconds after, I feel it in my throat. It’s a sense of tightness at the very base of my neck. I have no trouble breathing though.</p><p>When this happens I feel my pulse and realize the heart is beating crazy fast. The beat is much weaker than when I’m in a normal state, but it’s hellish fast.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/atrial-fibrillation-ecg.png" class="kg-image" alt loading="lazy" width="1052" height="634" srcset="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w600/2021/11/atrial-fibrillation-ecg.png 600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1000/2021/11/atrial-fibrillation-ecg.png 1000w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/atrial-fibrillation-ecg.png 1052w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><h3 id="speed">Speed</h3><p>At this point, I turn on the ECG on my watch and check the speed. Before starting on beta-blockers, I rated a speed of 160bpm a “good speed”, then anything above it was a “bad crisis”. I often had 170-180. I peaked at 202. Scary.</p><p>Now that I’m on medication for 2 weeks the top speed seems to be reduced to 160, which I now consider being a “bad crisis”. It often tops at 140-150 that became the “normal crisis”.</p><h3 id="duration">Duration</h3><p>Those crises last a variable amount of time. I experienced a few seconds and a few hours of constant crisis. Sometimes the speed keeps changing, moving through the entire range 60-160 without any sense, other times it keeps a constant speed throughout the entire episode.</p><p>After starting on beta-blockers I noticed a sensible reduction in the duration. Most of the crises last just one or two minutes now, but I still experience 45 minutes crises as well.</p><p>I observed some degree of connection between me lying down or being relaxed, and the beginning of a crisis. I also experienced crises right after sex. With or without my partner. But not always. I read that AF patients often lose confidence in having sex due to the crisis, and that really scares me. I’ll let you know because even if it is a very private matter, it is such an important part of everyone’s life.</p><p>Is it really necessary to say that I tried everything that came to mind to make them stop? Once I even tried dancing. It didn’t work. A doctor suggested the <strong><a href="https://themovementparadigm.com/valsava-maneuver/">Valsalva Maneuver</a></strong> maneuver, but it doesn’t seem to have any noticeable effect. If anything, it makes me feel super dizzy.</p><h2 id="life-during-a-crisis">Life During a Crisis</h2><p>I experience good and bad crises. Good crises don’t impact much, they are just like an annoying feeling and I can keep going with what I was doing when they started.</p><p>Then there are the bad ones. When that hit, I feel dizzy and my breath falls very short. I can’t talk much and feel quite weak. Sometimes, I feel truly cold.</p><p>The one time that my heart topped at 202bpm and the Swedish nurse decided it was a good time to send me to the ER, I had big troubles keeping up with her peace and had to ask to slow down her walking. I felt scared and embarrassed as I never had any troubles walking prior to that very day.</p><p>Fortunately, bad crises don’t happen so often. Unfortunately, when they kick in they usually stay with me for the longest time. Also, after a bad crisis, when the watch’s ECG tells me that I’m back to sinus rhythm, my heart keeps beating fast. It stays above 100bpm for hours. Again, now that I’m under medication the speed decreased, and after a bad crisis, I stay around 80bpm. It still takes hours to calm down.</p><h2 id="getting-a-visual">Getting a Visual</h2><p>Thanks to the Apple Watch I have my heartbeat constantly recorded in my iPhone’s Health App. I got to the point that I feel exposed that 20 minutes a day when the watch is charging… Am I going nut?</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/resting-heart-beat.png" class="kg-image" alt loading="lazy" width="2000" height="1202" srcset="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w600/2021/11/resting-heart-beat.png 600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1000/2021/11/resting-heart-beat.png 1000w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1600/2021/11/resting-heart-beat.png 1600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/2021/11/resting-heart-beat.png 2086w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption>My resting heartbeat decreased with a healthy lifestyle and with beta-blockers.</figcaption></figure><p>I keep an eye on the daily chart and feel very happy when I manage to go through 24 full hours without any crisis. Unfortunately, this doesn’t happen so often yet. I sincerely hope to witness improvements as I drop some weight, eat healthier food, train more, and adjust on my meds.</p><p>Then I look ad the hourly chart to track the progress with the crisis duration. Last week I was crazy happy as I noticed only a super-short crisis for the entire week. But then last night I got another 45-minute long one. It sucks.</p><p>Visualizing a few data points from my device helps me reason a bit around this situation. It doesn't exactly make it easier, just a bit more comprehensible.</p><p>Baby steps, I guess.</p>
