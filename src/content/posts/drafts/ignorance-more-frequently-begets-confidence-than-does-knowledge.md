<p>I came across this quote while reading about <a href="https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/dunning-kruger-effect/">the Dunning-Kruger effect</a>, thanks to a link posted by my good friend Johan.</p><p>Simply put, it explains why politicians and top managers are often perceived as idiots by most people. </p><blockquote>Less than average people have more than average confidence.</blockquote><p>This causes <strong>less than average confident people to blindly follow</strong> that first group, and I'm afraid that the less than average confident people are the great majority to walk around this little blue planet.</p><h2 id="the-influencer-effect">The Influencer Effect</h2><p>Having worked within the influencer world for a while, I can see the Dunning-Kruger effect clearly at play in Instagram or TikTok.</p><blockquote>For a <strong>few very prepared experts</strong> that share science-driven knowledge, we have to endure an <strong>endless list of mindless "influencers"</strong> that share mediocre content. </blockquote><p>From average-looking youngsters that employ heavy imagery filters to sell a wanna-fuck-me promise on their pages, to self-promoted experts that echo with each other on airplane stripes, 5g, vaccines...
