<p>This morning I found myself meditating on <strong>the role that luck plays in our lives</strong>, and two famous stories popped up in my head:</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">🪰 A fly desperately tries to escape through a closed window, repeatedly bashing against it until it dies, never noticing the open door just a few steps away.</blockquote><hr><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">🐁 Two mice fall into a bucket of milk; one gives up and dies, while the other kicks so much it accidentally turns the milk into butter and escapes.</blockquote><p>The morale of the two stories is somewhat a contradiction:</p><ul><li>🪰 Stop panicking and change what you are doing</li><li>🐁 Perseverance maketh success</li></ul><p>What intrigues me is that both the mice and the fly are panicking. They are kicking as hard as possible due to their lizard-brain response to a life-threatening situation.</p><p>However, the same behavior works in one story and leads to death in the other.</p><p>It appears that, no matter what you do, there is one ancient-wisdom-based story that proves you wrong. It's a vicious cycle right there.</p><p>So what?</p><p>No kicking effort would have saved their tail if the mice had fallen into water. So, the circumstance makes for the kick-ass mouse's survival. Not the effort. </p><p>Or at least, not the effort alone.</p><p>That is luck.</p>
