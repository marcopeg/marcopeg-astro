<p>I’ve just wrapped up my third week at Sinch, and the level of <em>domain-driven confusion</em> is rising fast. The deeper I get into the Telecom world—and how a large corporation implements it—the steeper the climb becomes.</p><p>But something interesting is happening. </p><p>Being new actually <strong><em>lets me ask some of those really basic questions</em></strong> that maybe haven’t been on the table for a while. </p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">“Why can CompetitorX do things<br>In a different way?”</blockquote><p>Honestly, I was a bit nervous tossing out that question. It could sound both arrogant and naïve… and in reality <strong><em>it was just me being curious.</em></strong></p><p>So you can imagine my relief when it was welcomed as an opportunity to revisit our decision-making history through fresh, updated lenses.</p><p>My boss even slacked me: <em>“Ask anything, don’t hold back.”</em><br>How nice.</p><hr><p>As it turns out, we do things the way we do <strong><em>because of choices made long ago</em></strong> in response to past events.</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">And then we just… <br>Never looked back</blockquote><p>Sounds familiar? It surely mirrors the origin story of most personal habits, too.</p><p>Luckily, I joined Sinch at a transformative moment—market signals, customer research, and internal sentiment pushed the company to <strong><em>focus on a single OKR</em></strong>: Customer Satisfaction.</p><p>This much is clear by week #3.</p><hr><h3 id="the-onboarding-high-level-tour">The Onboarding High-Level Tour</h3><p>Thanks to my predecessor, my boss, and our Tech Leads, the big picture is finally forming. They walked me through the system from broad strokes to the intricate web of dependencies an<em>d delightful Telecom edge cases.</em></p><p><strong>As an Architect,</strong> I feel like I’m back in math class: chalk everywhere, formulas on the board, no clarity in sight—but 20+ years of experience give me the confidence that it <em>will</em> click. <em>Ideas will come</em>.</p><p><strong>As an Engineering Manager,</strong> I’m about to start my 1-1s with the teams, peers, and the upper echelon of corporate management. <em>I feel the usual mix of excitement and trepidation.</em> </p><p>First impressions matter. And I remember well that some of my early impressions of past managers… were less than inspiring.</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">It always feels like<br><em>The First Time</em></blockquote><p>Five years of running 1-1s help, but it always feels like <em>“the first time,”</em> so I did my homework again. That’s how I stumbled upon<em> “the grumpiness question,” </em>which I decided to add to my personal template.</p><blockquote>If you’re one of my future 1-1s… please <a href="https://larahogan.me/blog/first-one-on-one-questions/" rel="noreferrer">don’t click this link</a>: let me surprise you. Everyone else — enjoy!</blockquote><p>I also want to thank fellow managers who shared their experience and scars with me. <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/claudio-caporro/" rel="noreferrer">Claudio</a>, in particular, made one thing crystal-clear: the Engineering Manager role is first and foremost a <strong><em>mission for personal support</em></strong>, with technical guidance and coordination layered on top—not the other way around.</p><p>Many of us have worked under managers who obsessed over “what needs to be done” and never got even remotely close to asking: <strong><em>“How are you doing today?”</em></strong></p><hr><h3 id="feelings-are-the-new-efficiency-knob">Feelings Are The New Efficiency Knob</h3><p>We now work with bleeding-edge AI tools that remove most of the traditional complexity of writing software. Coding is slowly becoming less of a human activity, while <em>context switching</em> and <em>ownership of AI output</em> are becoming the new craft.</p><p>In this environment, feelings aren’t a “soft factor.” Not anymore.</p><p>They are a <strong>primary driver of quality and velocity</strong>.</p><p>What happens when you’re grumpy? Tired? Off?</p><p>A year ago—pre-Vibe-Coding—one bad day affected <em>your</em> task.</p><p>Today, with constant reviews, parallel workflows, and pressure to do more, one bad day can ripple across multiple stories, features, and teams… ending in a chain of remediation meetings.</p><p>In this AI-augmented era, managers are increasingly <strong>risk controllers</strong> through <strong>human connection</strong>.</p><p>A warm question asked at the right time can prevent a very expensive butterfly effect.</p>
