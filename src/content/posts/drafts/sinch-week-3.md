<p>I’ve completed my third week at Sinch, and the level of <strong><em>domain-driven confusion</em></strong> is increasing steadily. The more involved I am in the Telecom domain and its implementation within a large corporation, the steeper my learning curve becomes.</p><p>But I also feel my external point of view and experience are becoming a great asset, as <strong><em>my beginner's mindset lets me ask questions</em></strong> that would otherwise come out as open challenges, or never be asked at all.</p><p>I’m particularly proud of this one: <em>“Why can CompetitorX do things differently from us?”</em> - I’ve been hearing that <em>CompetitorX</em> has much better APIs, or provides a much better onboarding experience for 15 full working days - but nobody ever explains to me “why”.</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt"><em>As it turns out, we do things the way we do because we started doing so in response to certain past events...<br>And never looked back!</em></blockquote><p>Does this sound familiar? <br>It surely matches most of my personal habits' origin stories!</p><p>Luckily, I joined Sinch during a transformative time when a cold shower from the market and research awakened the will to challenge the established patterns, <strong><em>with our customers' satisfaction as a single OKR</em></strong>.</p><p>This much is clear by week #3.</p><hr><p>A good deal of the high-level onboarding is done, thanks to my predecessor, my boss, and Tech Leads. They took the time to paint the big picture for me, starting with the broad strokes and refining it into an increasingly intricate web of dependencies and edge cases.</p><p><strong>As an Architect,</strong> I feel like I'm back in math class, with a lot of weird formulas on the blackboard, chalk covering my hands, and very little clarity in my mind. But I'm experienced enough to be confident that once the dust settles, ideas will come.</p><p><strong>As an Engineering Manager,</strong> I'm excited for the upcoming week(s) because I'll start the 1-1 with my teams. I'm going through a mix of anticipation and trepidation. How will they go? How can I come out as a supportive figure that they can trust?</p><p>After all, the first impression is hard to overcome, and I remember that my personal impression of quite a few of my line managers wasn't exactly pure gold.</p><p>I have my 5+ years of 1-1s on me, but it is always good to run some research, as it was the first time. And good people always share great content. During my searches, I stumbled upon <em>"the grumpiness question" </em>as the first one to shoot away. And I am seriously tempted to adopt it into my template.</p><p><em>So, if you are reading this and I'm your line manager, </em><a href="https://larahogan.me/blog/first-one-on-one-questions/" rel="noreferrer"><em>please don't click here</em></a><em> and save the spoil for our booked time! For everyone else, click on the link and enjoy!</em></p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">And great people always <br>Share great content</blockquote><p>I also want to thank a few fellow managers who recently took the time to share their experience with me, what worked, and what didn't.</p><p><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/claudio-caporro/" rel="noreferrer">Claudio</a> made it adamant that the EngMan must be approached as a personal support role with a share of technical guidance and task coordination, and not the other way around.</p><p>It's funny because we both share the experience of line managers who focused entirely on "what's to be done," never even getting close to the "how do you feel?".</p><hr><p>Today, more than ever, our feelings are the primary driver of quality and velocity. </p><p>We have bleeding-edge AI tools that remove most of the intrinsic complexity of a software engineer's tasks. </p><p>Writing code is less and less a human activity. Still, we are tasked with increasingly complex context-switching scenarios, where we are expected to take ownership of the AI's output through code reviews and context engineering.</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">Feelings are the primary driver of Quality and Velocity</blockquote><p>What happens when something makes you grumpy? Or when you feel under the weather?</p><p>Just a year ago, before the advent of Vibe Coding, under duress, one risked affecting only the task at hand. Fast forward ~9 months, and we are dividing our attention among multiple reviews, under constant pressure to do more with our time.</p><p>Today, the risk is far more spread out. One bad day can impact many stories, features, eventually cascading into God-only-knows how many remediation meetings!</p><p>In this new era of AI-driven Software Engineering, our job as managers is increasingly focused on <em>risk control through human touch</em>. A warm question asked at the right time may have greater effects than the famous <em>butterfly effect</em>.</p>
