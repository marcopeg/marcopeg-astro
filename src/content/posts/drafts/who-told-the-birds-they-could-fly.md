<p>History witnessed the very moment the first bird took off for the first time to undertake the first flight ever attempted in the entire known Universe. </p><p>It had never been done before and that bird could not know in advance whether it would work. </p><p>Until then, only short glides have been made by his peers and predecessors, always gliding down. No bird had ever managed to gain altitude on his own. In fact, it was common knowledge that such a feat could not be accomplished. </p><p>But on that particular day, that specific bird did it anyway. Putting his life at risk, challenging the common knowledge, and charging the sky with the confident belief he could make it. So he took off and flew.</p><p>I like to call that bird Orville.</p><hr><p>Some <a href="https://australian.museum/learn/dinosaurs/the-first-birds/">150 million years later</a>, two brothers set on a course to repeat that feat.</p><p><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers">Wilbur and Orville</a> were craftsmen who operated a somewhat successful bicycle repair shop, eventually starting their own brand of vehicles. But for them, that was not enough. That was just gliding downwards together with the flock. </p><p>Like the first bird at the beginning of our story, the Wright brothers sought after more challenging feats. </p><p>They were running after a dream that had no guarantee of turning into success. Staking odds against odds, they stubbornly kept coming back to their test site on Kitty Hawk season after season, uncomfortably sleeping in makeshift tents, fighting mosquitoes and adverse weather.</p><p>Above every other discomfort, they endured the laughs of the local folks, just like early Orville endured the laughs of his fellow – but less visionary – birds. </p><p>And just like Orville, they won. They took off. </p><p>In the middle of 1903's winter, they accomplished the first motor-operated flight. As they say, the rest is history.</p><hr><p>History witnesses plenty of first-timers: the first photograph, the first x-ray, and the first version of Angry Birds. The first computer, the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SixDegrees.com">first Social Network</a>, and the very first Internet-distributed porn movie. Some of those success stories are just the outcome of sheer luck – of being at the right place at the right moment (but even so, it did require a lot of effort to build the first version of <em>The Facebook</em>). </p><p>But for all the others, what did those first-timers have in common? What was the single ingredient without which it would have been impossible to win? What distinguishes Orville from all the other birds in the entire history of the universe?</p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">I call this trait:<br>"<em>The Humble Arrogance"</em></blockquote><p><em>"Humble Arrogance"</em> is the most arrogant oxymoron that I could find to humbly put into words the rules that I've set for myself over my first two decades of  adult life:</p><ol><li>I am the N.1 player in my life; I should trust my judgment.</li><li>I am not the only player in Life; I should listen to others, and be curious.</li><li>My decisions are based on my knowledge; I should keep on growing my knowledge.</li></ol><p>These three tenets are the base of my personal way of life. They help me build enough self-confidence to share my thoughts with you, and they keep my mindest open enough to appreciate your feedback as the precious treasure that it is; because any feedback fosters personal growth.</p><hr><p>Nobody told Orville he could fly. Nobody told the Wright brothers they could put wings around an engine. At the same time, everyone around encouraged them to try. </p><blockquote class="kg-blockquote-alt">You Can Do It!</blockquote><p>When you listen carefully, you hear the Universe whispering: "<em>You can do it.</em>" It keeps on giving you all the hints you need to build your own success. Those hints come in so many different forms though.</p><p>It could be a dear friend or partner highlighting your capabilities or predisposition toward a particular feat, or you could hear the right song at the right moment. Did you ever try listening to "<em><a href="https://open.spotify.com/track/2KH16WveTQWT6KOG9Rg6e2">The eye of the tiger</a></em>" the very moment you were about to give up on your daily training session?</p><p>Thomas Edison grew in fame because he was capable of hearing "<em>you can do it</em>" in every failed attempt to build the light bulb. And as the story goes, he failed over two thousand times!</p><p>Was he plain stupid to keep going like that? Was it sheer stubbornness? </p><p>I don't think so. </p><p>I believe Edison was an <em>Humble Arrogant</em>.</p><p>He certainly build plenty of knowledge for himself, he trusted his own judgment regarding a possible and relatively new application of electricity and kept an open mind on new feedback flowing in from every possible direction.</p><p>Where many see "failure" he saw "feedback". And he kept on using that feedback to evolve his idea. He knew he couldn't do it alone, and he knew how to use help. It just happened that most of the help he needed came from previous attempts.</p><p>After all, experience is just the Universe helping you out.</p>
