# Marco Peg's Blog Image Style Guide

This style guide captures the visual identity of **Marco Pegoraro's blog ([marcopeg.com](https://marcopeg.com))**, extracted directly from published posts and historical materials (2015–2026).

Marco's blog is conversational, vulnerable, opinionated, and rooted in the engineering trenches. The imagery must reflect that same personality: **witty, relatable, cinematic, and never corporate**.

---

## 🎨 The 3 Core Visual Archetypes

Marco's blog uses three distinct visual archetypes. Every generated hero image must clearly belong to one of these three:

### 1. The Pop Culture / Cinema Remix (Signature Move)
Marco constantly weaves pop culture and cinema into his technical essays. The hero images reflect this by taking iconic movie scenes, posters, or cultural tropes and giving them an engineering or AI twist.
- **Real Examples in Repo**:
  - `50-first-ai-tasks.jpg`: The iconic *50 First Dates* movie poster (Adam Sandler & Drew Barrymore on the beach), but Drew's face is replaced with a retro cartoon robot emoji (`🤖`) because AI bots suffer from memory loss!
  - `the-godfather.png`: The Don Vito Corleone hand-kissing scene representing authority, hierarchy, and organizational politics.
  - `boondock-saints.jpeg`: The two MacManus brothers aiming guns down the lens representing team camaraderie facing Friday night outages together (*The Curse of the Proud Developer*).
- **Prompt Formula**:
  `[Iconic movie scene or classic cinema poster homage: e.g. Pulp Fiction, Men in Black, Top Gun, Matrix, Back to the Future] + [Tech / AI subversion: swapping a character with a quirky robot, replacing weapons with Ethernet cables or mechanical keyboards] + [Cinematic film still lighting, 35mm film grain, 16:9 aspect ratio]`.

### 2. The Humorous Developer Caricature
Expressive, comic/editorial illustrations that capture the visceral reality of software engineering—stress, relief, absurdity, and triumph.
- **Real Examples in Repo**:
  - `DALL-E-2024-02-28-10.42.38...webp`: An overwhelmed, wide-eyed software engineer with disheveled hair, thick round glasses, funny t-shirt, surrounded by chaotic monitors with code, desk toys, and empty coffee mugs.
  - `DALL-E-2024-02-28-11.43.16...webp`: A jubilant developer leaning back in his ergonomic chair with a triumphant grin, morning sun streaming through huge windows onto green Swedish countryside, steaming coffee on the desk.
- **Style Markers**:
  - Exaggerated expressions (comical panic or beaming satisfaction).
  - Environmental storytelling: tangled wires, multiple code monitors, mechanical keyboards, espresso mugs, rubber ducks.
  - Vibrant, rich editorial cartoon or comic book art style (Pixar/comic hybrid, expressive linework, warm lighting).

### 3. The Tangible Ironic Metaphor / Real-World Object
Using tangible, everyday physical objects to make an abstract computing concept feel grounded and funny.
- **Real Examples in Repo**:
  - `it-is-better-to-be-late.png`: A vintage weathered wooden plaque reading *"It's better to be late than to arrive ugly."*
  - `books-1.png`: Physical book covers grouped on a wooden table.
  - `the-era-for-self-developing-apps.png`: A humanoid robot physically hunched over a standard office desk typing on a real mechanical keyboard chatting with users.

## 🚫 What Marco's Blog NEVER Uses (Banned Styles)

- ❌ **Generic Corporate 3D Renders**: Clean white plastic mannequins, generic blue spheres, floating abstract cubes.
- ❌ **Stock Cybersecurity Clichés**: Anonymous hooded hackers in binary code rain, generic padlock graphics, blue wireframe hands touching glass.
- ❌ **Impersonal Tech-Bro Aesthetics**: Sleek sterile corporate offices without a soul.
- ❌ **Boring Stock Photography**: Unedited generic photos of people in business suits pointing at laptops.

---

## 📐 Composition & Color Rules

1. **Aspect Ratio**: Always **16:9** (or 3:2) widescreen format (e.g. 1376x768, 1536x1024, 1920x1080).
2. **Visual Anchor & Negative Space**: Keep the main subject offset to one side (left or center), leaving breathing room or negative space on the opposite side for titles and card scanning.
3. **Warmth & Contrast**: Even in dark tech scenes, include warm human touches—warm amber lighting, a steaming ceramic coffee mug, wooden desktop grain, or golden sunlight.
4. **Humor & Irony**: If a prompt doesn't have at least one touch of humor, irony, or storytelling tension, rewrite it!
